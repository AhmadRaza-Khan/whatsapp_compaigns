To connect Bird WhatsApp to your backend, you need an API key for sending and a webhook endpoint for receiving replies and delivery updates.

The flow is: Your application → your backend → Bird API → WhatsApp, with events sent back to your backend through webhooks.

1. Set up your WhatsApp sender
For a quick outbound test: Bird’s managed templates let you send from a shared Bird number without connecting your own number. These shared numbers do not route customer replies to your workspace.
For messaging under your own business number and receiving replies: connect your WhatsApp number to Bird through Meta’s signup.
If you already use the WhatsApp Business app, check the coexistence setup to keep using the app alongside Bird, where eligible. Don’t disconnect your existing setup just to start integration.
2. Create a backend API key
In Platform tools → API keys, create a workspace key with whatsapp:write scope.

Store it in your backend’s environment variables or secret manager—never in frontend code or a mobile app. Bird’s quickstart covers key creation.

3. Send messages from your backend
Your workspace context lists the EU region, so use:

POST https://eu1.platform.bird.com/v1/whatsapp/messages

Authenticate using a Bearer API key and send JSON containing:

to: the customer’s phone number in international format, such as +….
template: an approved template’s slug and any required variables.
from: your connected sender when sending your own template or a free-form message. Omit it for Bird-managed templates.
Use an approved template to start a conversation. Free-form messages are allowed within the customer’s open 24-hour service window.

Source: Send a WhatsApp message API reference.

4. Receive replies and delivery updates
Create a webhook endpoint in your backend and register it through Bird’s Webhooks page. Subscribe to events such as:

whatsapp.received — customer replies
whatsapp.delivered — delivery confirmation
whatsapp.failed and whatsapp.rejected — unsuccessful sends
Implement webhook signature verification before processing events. See WhatsApp webhooks.

5. Save the message ID and test end to end
Bird returns 202 Accepted with a message ID. Save that ID against your application’s record; acceptance is not delivery confirmation. Use webhook events to update the final status.

Test sending, delivery updates, and—when using your own connected number—an inbound reply before going live.





To send WhatsApp messages from your own number, first connect your Meta WhatsApp Business Account and number to Bird through Meta Embedded Signup. After that, your backend sends messages through Bird’s API—you don’t need to pass a Meta access token with each send.

The exact onboarding path depends on how your number is currently used.

1. Prepare your business and number
Have these ready:

A Meta login with permission to manage your business.
Your existing Meta business portfolio, or the business details needed to create one.
Your own phone number.
Access to receive an SMS or voice verification code for standard registration.
The phone registration PIN, if your number already has one.
Meta business verification and phone verification are different: you can connect and start sending before Meta verifies your business, but the number must complete its applicable verification process. Verification explained

2. Connect your number using the correct path
If your number is not currently registered with WhatsApp
Open WhatsApp → Numbers in Bird.
Choose New number → Set up a number.
Choose I have my own number.
Enter your number and complete Meta’s SMS or voice-code verification.
Continue through Meta’s signup:
Sign in to Meta.
Select or create your business portfolio.
Select or create the WhatsApp Business Account (WABA) for your business.
Provide the requested business details.
Review and confirm the access Bird requests.
Complete Meta’s final confirmation and return to Bird.
Wait until the number shows Connected before sending.
Bird’s illustrated guide uses a Bird-allocated, pre-verified number, so its automatic verification steps differ from an external number you own. Own-number options · Meta signup guide

If your number is already in the WhatsApp Business app
Use coexistence, where eligible, to keep the app working alongside Bird:

In Bird’s WhatsApp Numbers page, choose New number → Keep using the Business app.
Enter a name and select Connect WhatsApp.
In Meta’s popup, enter your Business app number and select or create your business portfolio.
When the QR code appears, open WhatsApp Business on your phone: Settings → Account → Business Platform → Connect to the Business Platform.
Scan the QR code and choose whether to share chat history.
Return to the desktop popup, confirm your WhatsApp account, and approve Bird’s requested permissions.
Finish Meta’s setup, then check that Bird shows Connected.
This route requires WhatsApp Business 2.24.17 or later. Keep your existing connection while setup is pending. Coexistence walkthrough

If the number is in personal WhatsApp or already connected to another API provider, don’t delete or disconnect it yet. Its current registration determines the supported transition.

3. Prepare a template for your own sender
For a business-initiated message, use an approved template belonging to the same WABA as your connected number.

Do not use Bird-managed bird_… templates for this goal: they send from shared Bird numbers, not your own number.

Use your own approved template’s slug and supply its required variables. Free-form text is allowed only during the customer’s open 24-hour service window. Sending requirements

4. Connect your backend to Bird
Create a workspace API key under Platform tools → API keys with whatsapp:write scope.
Store it only in your backend’s secret manager or environment.
For your EU workspace, send requests to: POST https://eu1.platform.bird.com/v1/whatsapp/messages
Authenticate with the Bird key as a Bearer token.
Include:
from: your connected business phone number, in international +… format.
to: the customer’s number.
template: your own approved template’s slug and required variables.
The from number must belong to your workspace and to the same WABA as the template. API reference

5. Add webhooks and test end to end
Register your backend webhook through Bird’s Webhooks page and implement signature verification. Subscribe to:

whatsapp.received for customer replies.
whatsapp.delivered for delivery confirmation.
whatsapp.failed and whatsapp.rejected for unsuccessful sends.
Test one approved template to your own phone, confirm the sender is your business number, and check the delivery event. Save the message ID: 202 Accepted means accepted, not delivered. Also check the organization wallet and the connected number’s sending limits before production use. WhatsApp webhooks
